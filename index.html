Maintenance is an investment in the car you already own. Planned care helps protect its reliability, catches wear earlier, and gives it a better chance to stay on the road longer.

The maintenance mindset

Protect the miles you have already paid for.

Small, consistent upkeep can be easier to plan for than a preventable breakdown. Think of every inspection, fluid check, and service record as protection for your car’s next mile.

  • Preserve reliabilityHelp key systems keep doing their job.
  • Catch wear soonerAddress warning signs before they grow.
  • Support long-term valueKeep a clear record of consistent care.

01 Regular oil changes

Use the oil type and interval listed for your vehicle. Many cars need service somewhere between 3,000 and 7,500 miles, but the owner’s manual is the source of truth.

Why it pays: Fresh oil protects moving engine parts and helps limit avoidable wear.

02 Check tire pressure

Check tires when they are cold, about once a month and before long trips. Use the pressure on the driver-door label, not the number molded into the tire.

Why it pays: Correct pressure supports safer handling and helps your tread wear more evenly.

03 Battery care

Keep terminals clean and connections secure. Have the battery tested before temperature extremes or when starts become slow.

Why it pays: A quick check can reduce the chance of an inconvenient no-start.

04 Coolant & fluids

Check coolant, brake, transmission, power-steering, and washer fluids as applicable. Investigate unexpected drops instead of repeatedly topping them off, and never open a hot cooling system.

Why it pays: Proper fluids help cool, lubricate, and protect expensive systems.

05 Brake inspections

Do not ignore grinding, vibration, pulling, a soft pedal, or a change in stopping distance. Have concerns inspected promptly; if braking feels unsafe, stop driving and arrange service.

Why it pays: Early attention can protect more of the braking system—and your safety.

06 Follow the schedule

Use the normal or severe-duty schedule in your owner’s manual based on how and where you drive.

Why it pays: Timely service helps you plan upkeep before wear turns into downtime.

07 Rotate and align tires

Rotate tires at the interval recommended for your vehicle. If it pulls, the steering wheel sits off-center, or wear is uneven, have the alignment checked.

Why it pays: Even wear can help you get more useful life from a full set of tires.

08 Replace filters

Inspect the engine air filter and cabin filter on schedule, especially after dusty driving or heavy pollen seasons.

Why it pays: Clean filters support engine airflow and a more effective heating and cooling system.

09 Inspect belts and hoses

Look for cracks, glazing, bulges, leaks, or frayed edges. Chirping sounds, coolant smells, or visible damage deserve attention.

Why it pays: Replacing a worn part on your schedule is better than waiting for it to fail on the road.

10 Test lights and wipers

Walk around the car to test exterior lights. Replace wipers that streak, chatter, or leave gaps before bad weather arrives.

Why it pays: Clear visibility helps protect you, your car, and everyone sharing the road.

11 Wash away road salt

During and after winter, rinse road salt from the body, wheel wells, and undercarriage when temperatures allow.

Why it pays: Removing corrosive residue helps protect metal, fasteners, and brake components.

12 Keep service records

Save receipts and record dates, mileage, parts, and upcoming work in a notebook or phone note.

Why it pays: A clear history prevents duplicate work, supports diagnosis, and documents consistent care.

13 Act on warning lights

Read the owner’s manual when a warning appears. A flashing check-engine light or red oil-pressure or temperature warning can require stopping safely and shutting the engine off.

Why it pays: A quick response can keep a developing problem from causing more damage.

Intervals vary by vehicle and driving conditions. Follow your owner’s manual and ask a qualified technician when you are unsure. Have a general question? Call us or stop by the lot.
